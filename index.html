Free certificate course claims, 2026

Fifteen courses advertised as free with a certificate, checked against what the checkout page actually said.

Every course in this set markets itself as free and every one of them gives you access to the material without paying. That is not the same as a free certificate. The audit records the price field, the clause that names the charge, and the date each claim was last checked, so a reader can see whether "free" means free to study or free to certify.

The data

The table is published in two formats, both generated from the same rows:

  • free-certificate-courses.csv — one row per course, flat columns.
  • free-certificate-courses.json — the same rows with the evidence fields kept separate.

Both are released under CC BY 4.0. The readable version, with the table rendered and the method described, is the dataset page. The three questions that settle a claim in five minutes are on the online courses guide, and the same questions applied to AI tools are on the free AI tools hub.

What counts as evidence

A row only keeps "free certificate" if the certificate can be issued without a payment step: no certificate fee, no paid exam, and no subscription that has to be live at the moment the certificate is released. A course that is free to study but gates the certificate is recorded as charging, with the clause quoted, because that is the part a learner discovers last.
